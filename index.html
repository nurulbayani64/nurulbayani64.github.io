Scenario Background

Predicting Peak Fertilizer & Irrigation Demand via Sigmoid Growth Modelling

In Perlis, Malaysia, the premium Harumanis mango (MA 128) undergoes a highly regulated 84-day growth cycle from fruit set to physiological maturity. For an agrotechnologist, knowing how heavy a mango is on Day \( t \) is useful, but knowing how fast it is growing (the rate of change, \( f'(t) \)) is critical.

The peak growth rate (\( f''(t) = 0 \)) marks the absolute highest demand for potassium (K) fertilization and precise water scheduling to prevent premature fruit drop or skin cracking. Below, we utilize a customized Verhulst-Logistic Growth Model calibrated with empirical research data from MARDI and the Department of Agriculture Perlis to simulate and analyze these critical thresholds.

The Mathematics of Growth

Primal Function \( f(t) \) (Weight in grams):
f(t) = \frac{L}{1 + e^{-k(t - t_0)}}
Derivative \( f'(t) \) (Growth Rate in g/day):
f'(t) = k \cdot f(t) \cdot \left(1 - \frac{f(t)}{L}\right)
*This states that growth rate is proportional to both current size and remaining carrying capacity.
Parameters used:
\( L = 480\text{g} \) Max weight
\( k = 0.10 \) Growth constant
\( t_0 = 42\text{d} \) Inflection point

Derivatives Breakdown for Agrotechnologists

Step 1: Finding the Rate of Change via Calculus

To calculate the actual instantaneous expansion rate of the Harumanis fruit, we take the mathematical derivative of our logistic model \( f(t) \) with respect to time \( t \):

$$ f'(t) = \frac{d}{dt} \left[ \frac{L}{1 + e^{-k(t-t_0)}} \right] $$ $$ = \frac{L \cdot k \cdot e^{-k(t-t_0)}}{(1 + e^{-k(t-t_0)})^2} $$

This complex quotient/chain rule can be beautifully simplified directly by substituting \( f(t) \), yielding the state-dependent differential equation: \( f'(t) = k \cdot f(t) \cdot (1 - \frac{f(t)}{L}) \).

Step 2: Bio-Interpretation of Derivatives

In bioscience engineering, a derivative is never just an abstract formula; it represents physical resource transformation rates:

  • 1 Positive Derivative (\( f'(t) > 0 \)): The fruitlet is actively drawing sucrose, potassium, and water. A high value suggests extreme physiological demand.
  • 2 Zero Derivative (\( f'(t) \approx 0 \)): The fruit has plateaued at mature physical capacity, triggering the biochemical process of ripening. Ready to harvest!
  • 3 Inflection Point (\( f''(t) = 0 \)): The peak growth speed. In the case of Harumanis, this happens exactly at Day 42, showing a maximum daily accumulation of 12 grams per day!
